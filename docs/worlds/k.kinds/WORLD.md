# k.kinds: one kind-of relation, followed upwards by one rule, and the up edges of the catalog

A module with one type and two rules, no facts of its own (Q34 item 6). `k.kinds.kind` has a `name`, `is_a` (the
kinds right above, many) and `kind_of` (every kind above, derived). The rules: what a thing is, it is a kind of; a
kind of a kind of something is a kind of it. `k.commonsense.thing`, `k.people.kind` and `k.shapes.shape` extend
`k.kinds.kind`, so one rule follows their chains: a square is a kind of rectangle, rhombus, quadrilateral and
polygon (Q33: `is_a` of a shape now holds many); a grandfather is a kind of man; milk, a drink of `k.food`, is a
liquid by a rule of `k.commonsense` and so a kind of liquid. The derived `kind_of` lands on the wire of the thing
itself (its type is the world's own type).

Not taken in: `k.words.sense` (WordNet's chains, keyed by word, part of speech and sense, not by name; its import
already writes `kind_of`, so the key is the same) and `k.parts.inherited.from` (a table of where a part was inherited
from, computed by its tool). Checked by `tests/shared-modules.test.mjs`.

## The up edges of the catalog (`k.kinds.parent`, reviewer 21:55)

For the library's `cousins()` (a case is lifted 1-3 layers up the abstraction graph and instantiated down again),
the abstraction space is a layered graph, not a tree: a thing has several parents on several dimensions. Every such
edge the catalog has is one fact `k.kinds.parent name=<thing> is_a=<parent> dimension=<d>` with the fact it is read
from (`from` a type, `key`, `value`; `group` for a drawing's source group) and that fact's `core.source`. They are
generated by `tools/kinds-core.mjs` (part of `npm run build`) from the other worlds; nothing is typed per thing.

| dimension | read from | example |
|---|---|---|
| `kind` | k.animals class, k.attributes kind, k.food kind, k.body kind, k.space kind, k.wearables kind, k.people role is and group; WordNet hypernyms of the name's sense in k.words and the chain above; the group of a drawing's source (k.objects shards, table `GROUPS`); the head of a name of several words | cow: mammal, cattle (WordNet); car: vehicle, motor vehicle; Christmas cake: cake; tangerine: fruit (Noto group) |
| `place` | k.animals habitat | cow: farm animal |
| `feature` | k.animals legs and covering; k.attributes wheels; k.parts has (wheels, an engine or motor, wings) | cow: animal with legs; car: thing with wheels, machine |
| `use` | k.attributes used_for; k.wearables worn_on | car: thing for travelling; hat: thing worn on the head |
| `material` | k.attributes material | car: thing made of metal |
| `diet` | k.animals diet | cow: herbivore |
| `part_of` | k.parts has and plain (whole, inherited, usual), k.parts called part, k.body part_of (derived, Q59) | wheel, car wheel, door: car; leg: ant; eyebrow: face |
| `origin` | k.food comes_from and grows_on (derived, Q59) | beef: cow; cheese: milk; apple: tree; carrot: underground |

The kinds these edges make (farm animal, thing with wheels, road vehicle, machine) get parents of their own (shard
`classes`), so every thing reaches its root in 1-3 layers. The graph is `k.kinds.parent` plus the `is_a` of the worlds
that extend `k.kinds.kind` (k.commonsense, k.people kind and role, k.shapes), which are not copied. No rule reads
`k.kinds.parent`: `kind_of` stays the chain of `is_a`.

Q59: the `part_of` and `origin` edges are derived at build, never copied by hand: `from`, `key`, `value` name the
source fact (a k.parts, k.body or k.food fact), which stays the one home; they are added after every kind edge and
only for a pair no other edge relates. Going up a part_of edge gives cousins (a wheel, part of a car; the other parts
of a car; up from the car to the vehicles and down to their wheels).

Shards by dimension and licence: `kind`, `classes`, `head`, `place`, `feature`, `use`, `material`, `diet`, `part_of`,
`origin`, `group-hand` (CC0, from the catalog's own facts); `wordnet-1`, `wordnet-2`, `feature-wordnet`,
`part_of-wordnet-*` (WordNet-3.0);
`group-noto` (Apache-2.0 and Unicode-3.0), `group-mulberry`, `group-openmoji` (CC-BY-SA-4.0).

How the WordNet sense of a name is chosen: the first noun sense under the root of the sense the catalog gives the name
(an animal of k.animals: animal; a food: food, foodstuff, beverage...; a drawing filed under animals: animal; else a
thing: artifact, natural object, else the first concrete sense; a rare word whose senses have no chain: its first
sense). A word of a chain is the first word of a hypernym synset, so a chain edge is kept only where that word's
chosen sense has that parent (the chain of "carriage", a railway car, does not make "car" a wheeled vehicle). Names
are words, so two senses of one name share a node; Q59 (3): on the edges of a name with two senses in the catalog
(k.attributes.same only=false: turkey the bird and the food, crane the bird and the machine) `sense` says which sense
the edge is about (the sense whose types hold the fact it is read from; for a WordNet edge the sense that chose the
WordNet sense), and is part of the edge's identity (cod is a fish as an animal and as a food: two edges). Edges read
from facts keyed by word (k.parts) carry none. Bat is one sense in the catalog (no club), so it has none.

The head of a name ("cloud with rain" is a cloud, "DVD player" a player): the last word before with, without, at, of,
in, on, for or behind, singular where needed, when it is a thing of the catalog or a WordNet noun under the root;
not for names with "and", proper names, Mulberry-only names (the qualifier comes last: "chair computer") and a short
list of compounds that are not their head (guinea pig, teddy bear, Komodo dragon). A sample of 100 before that list
had 6 wrong.

The report `PARENTS.md` (generated) gives the numbers, the things with no parent, and the things with one parent by
the worlds that name them, with where a plain second parent would come from. Checked by `tests/k.kinds.test.mjs`.
## Selectional restrictions: what roles and relations take (block "21:05" item 1)

For a reader that assembles a frame from the content words by the types of the roles (the library's type-driven
reading), two kinds of facts, written by `tools/relations-core.mjs` (part of `npm run build`):

- **`k.kinds.argument`** (shard `arguments`, 18 classes): thing, kind, place, direction, value, attribute, amount,
  number, name, person, group, topic, means (the values of `k.actions.role holds`: every role of every act names
  one), and act, time, word, text, boolean (classes of the objects of relations). Each names the catalog `type`s
  whose things fill it, `live=true` where a thing of the live world fills it too, the `sense`s it takes (those of
  `tools/joins.mjs`: animal, food, plant, person, body part, clothing, colour, shape, place, time, body in space,
  thing) and examples. "Send it to Ann": the recipient holds `person`, so k.people kinds and roles and the people
  of the live world fill it, a colour does not.
- **`k.kinds.relation`** (shards `relations-<world>`, one per knowledge world, 919): every key of every type of every knowledge world, read as a
  relation, with its `subject` (the kind of the type in a word: animal, country; `word` for types keyed by the word,
  `thing` for types keyed by a drawing), `subject_sense`, and its `object`: the class, `object_type` (the types
  whose things the values name, the best first), `object_sense`, and `scale` (the k.values scale of an enum).
  "What sound does a cow make": `k.animals.animal` `sound`, subject animal, object a word (k.words); "the capital of
  Peru": `k.geo.country` `capital`, subject country (place), object a place, `k.geo.city`; "how many legs":
  `legs`, a number (its unit in `k.units.measure`); "what colour is a banana": `k.attributes.thing` `colour`, a
  value, k.colours / k.values colours; "can a penguin fly": `can`, an act, `k.actions.action`.

**Declared joins first (Q61).** A slot whose values name things of another type declares the join (`type=T by=k`,
sopworld's join, checked per value and open while no `T` with that value is loaded): the relation takes `T` as its
object type, `found=declared` and `object_by=k`. Only where no join is declared does the rule below apply, and a type
found by the value share is marked `found=value`: a suggestion. `docs/worlds/k.kinds/JOINS-REPORT.md` (generated by
`tools/joins-report.mjs`, part of `npm run build`) checks every declared join against every shard (values that meet
a target, open values, broken joins) and lists the undeclared keys the value share joins to a type;
`tests/k.kinds.joins-report.test.mjs` warns on them and never fails because another world grew, and the relations test
compares only the declared and schema facts strictly (value-share facts that differ are a warning).

Otherwise the object is found by rule: a slot `type=` names it; a boolean, a number (an amount where `k.units.measure` gives a
unit), an enum (a value, with a scale if its steps are a k.values scale) or a free string (text) say their class;
keys of a kind (`is_a`, `kind`, `class`) and of a place (`place`, `at`, `habitat`) say theirs; every other key is
joined by value: the types whose naming key holds at least half of its distinct values (regular plurals read as
singulars), at most three, within nine tenths of the best share; types keyed by the word whatever it means (k.words,
k.parts) only when no other joins, so a key of English words is a `word`. A key that joins nothing is a `word` too.

Measured (`tests/k.kinds.relations.test.mjs`): every role of k.actions holds a class; every key of every
knowledge type has a relation (919: 182 word, 151 number, 142 value, 120 thing, 84 text, 77 amount, 50 boolean,
38 kind, 24 place, 24 time, 22 act, 3 group, 2 person); 28 relations of the first host's question frames take
the subject and object they must; the 74 keys that the readings of the 292 real questions reach
(`tools/ask-real.mjs`) all have a relation, and none of a named class lacks its object type.

Known limits: a relation is typed by the values the catalog has today, so a key whose values join no world (the
young of an animal: kit, cygnet; WordNet's common words lack most) is a `word`; the subject of a type keyed by a
drawing is any thing of that name; the senses of `k.objects.object` depend on the shard (no fixed sense).

## Structures of worlds, for alignment (block "21:05" item 4)

For the library's transfer by analogy: each world schema, and each knowledge world whose things a live world makes
or reads, says which **structures** it is and which of its types, keys and acts plays each **role** (shard
`structures`, written by `tools/structures-core.mjs`, 231 wires).

| structure | roles (the first two needed) | worlds |
|---|---|---|
| assignment (items in slots under constraints) | item, slot, assigned, container, slot_of, capacity, order, constraint | w.seating, w.timeline, k.layouts |
| whole with parts | whole, part, part_of, part_kind, position, size, order | w.face, w.document, w.chart, k.objects, k.faces, k.parts, k.scenes, k.genres, k.geo |
| sequence | element, position, sequence, element_of, end, start, finish, next | w.timeline, w.list, w.document, k.time, k.story, k.genres, k.procedures |
| graph (nodes and links) | node, link, from, to, label | w.seating, w.timeline, k.layouts, k.objects, k.story, k.procedures, k.geo |
| placement (things in a space) | thing, position, space, layer, size | w.seating, w.face, w.map, k.layouts, k.faces, k.scenes |
| values by category | value, category, series, value_of, unit, order | w.chart |

`k.kinds.structure` (name, about, role, needs), `k.kinds.role` (structure, name, about), `k.kinds.form` (world,
structure, about: how the world is that structure) and `k.kinds.cast` (world, structure, role, filler, `as` type /
key / act: `w.seating` assignment item = `w.seating.guest`, assigned = `w.seating.guest.seat`; graph link = the act
`connect`). 17 worlds, 33 forms, 153 casts. Aligning two worlds of one structure role by role gives the analogy (a
guest in a seat ~ an event at a time ~ a thing in a slot of a layout; a drawing's part ~ a document's chapter ~ a
face's part); the roles one world casts and the other does not are what a transfer may offer (a timeline's event has
an end, a start and a finish of its timeline; a list has none). Measured in `tests/k.kinds.structures.test.mjs`: 96
pairs of worlds share a structure, 343 filler pairs aligned, 174 roles offered for transfer.

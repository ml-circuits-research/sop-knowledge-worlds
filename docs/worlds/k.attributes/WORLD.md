# k.attributes

The attributes of kinds of things: which attributes a kind of thing usually has, where the values of each attribute
are found in the worlds, and the usual values of common things that no other world says.

## What it knows

- `k.attributes.attribute` (Round 11: also length, height, distance, temperature, weight, named as the scales of `k.values`, with the keys that hold the numbers): colour, material, size, place, kind, parts, legs, wheels, can, cannot, lives, eats,
  covering, sound, used_for, with `holds` and `about`.
- `k.attributes.found` (identity `attribute`, `type`, `key`): where the values are: the colour of a food is
  `k.food.food.colour`, of a common thing `k.attributes.thing.colour`, of each part of a drawing
  `k.objects.part.colour`; the size and place of a drawable thing are `k.scenes.thing.size` and `.place`; legs,
  habitat (lives), eats, covering, sound, can and cannot of an animal are in `k.animals.animal`. 26 facts; a test
  checks that every world, type and key named exists. Values are not copied between worlds.
- `k.attributes.kind`: 26 kinds of things (animal, plant, vehicle, building, furniture, clothing, tool, container,
  toy, instrument, device …) and the attributes each usually has.
- `k.attributes.thing` (identity `name`): 761 common things with `kind` (688), `colour` (256; one of the eleven
  basic colours, many: a zebra is black and white), `material` (423), `wheels` (47; 0 for a boat or a sledge),
  `can` (242: fly, swim, float, roll, move, grow, burn, melt, shine, ring …), `used_for` (459). Every fact is
  `named=model`: the values were given by a language model from the name of the thing, each only where it is
  plain. **A missing key says nothing.** `colour=any` (116 things: a car, a ball, a shirt, a book) says that the
  thing comes in any colour and has no usual one; it never stands next to a colour.
- `k.attributes.drawn` (identity `name`): for 2,103 drawable things of `k.objects` the main colours of the drawing,
  the largest first, at most three: `drawn_colour` (one of the eleven basic colours) and `drawn_shade` (the nearest
  colour name of `k.values.colour`: silver, mustard, light sky blue). Computed by `tools/drawn-colour.mjs` from the
  parts as they are drawn (a grid of 60 × 60 places, the topmost filled part at each; a colour is kept from a fifth
  of the filled places). It says how the thing is drawn, not its usual colour: the two facts are never mixed. A
  host that needs a colour takes `k.attributes.thing.colour`, then `drawn_colour`. Five drawings made of lines only
  (water, conical flask, tripod, backstop, goal) have none.

## Switches and defaults (block 07:40 item 4)

- `k.attributes.switch`: 9 attributes of the things a user makes that are true or false, with the verbs that turn
  them on (`on`) and off (`off`) and their value before any act (`default`): visible (show, unhide, reveal / hide;
  true), stacked (stack / unstack), highlighted, selected (select / deselect, unselect), locked, pinned, grouped
  (group / ungroup), switched on (switch on, turn on / switch off, turn off), bold (bold, embolden / unbold); all
  false by default but visible. One home per pair: hidden is visible read backwards.
- `default_type` and `default_key` on an attribute say where the value it goes back to on "reset" is found: the
  colour of each part in the drawing (`k.objects.part colour`), the usual size of a drawable thing
  (`k.scenes.thing size`). The verb `reset` of k.actions (act change) says `value=default`.

## Aspects

- `k.attributes.aspect`: visual, spatial, relational, numeric, textual, geographic, temporal, social, with the
  views that show each where that is plain (`shown_in`: visual: picture, card; relational: diagram; numeric:
  chart; geographic: map; textual: document, card). Every attribute names its `aspect`.
- `k.attributes.family`: 16 families of things: common thing (the 1,500 frequent nouns for physical things),
  animal, food, country, city, river, mountain, mountain range, continent, kind of person, role, month, weekday,
  season, duration, body in space; each with its world, type and the key that names the thing.
- `k.attributes.shows` (identity `family`, `type`, `key`): a family shows an aspect through this key of this type
  (`by`: the key that holds the name of the thing). 131 facts. "Which aspects does a mountain have": relational,
  numeric, geographic; "where is the numeric aspect of a country": `k.geo.country`, keys population and area.
- **Coverage table:** [ASPECTS.md](ASPECTS.md), family × aspect, generated by `tools/aspects-report.mjs`: how many
  things of each family have at least one value of each aspect.

## Numbers of common things

- `k.attributes.number` (identity `name`, Round 10 item 5): the usual numbers of 975 of the 1,500 common things,
  model-labelled (`named=model`) from the everyday meaning of the name, each only where plain: `size_about` (the
  longest side, metres; 975), `weight_about` (kilograms; 855), `speed_about` (usual top speed, km/h, for what moves
  by itself or carries people, animals among them; 232), `legs` of things that are not animals (14), `sides` (8),
  `price_class` (cheap, medium, expensive; 645). `weight_class` and `speed_class` are computed from the numbers by
  `tools/numbers-core.mjs` (light under 2 kg, heavy over 30 kg; slow under 10 km/h, fast over 60 km/h); the three
  classes are steps of the `k.values` scales weight, speed and price, so "heavier", "fastest" and "cheaper" lead
  through `k.values.comparative` and `k.attributes.found` to these keys. Units are in `k.units.measure`.
- Left out on purpose: substances and drinks (honey, milk), places and landforms whose size is not one number
  (street, island, valley), bodies in space (`k.space` has them); the weight of buildings.
- Table `sources/hand-core/numbers.tsv`; audit `sources/hand-core/numbers-audit.tsv`.
- Q37: `sense` (identity with `name`, only where the name has several senses in `k.attributes.same`): which one the
  numbers are about, a value of `k.attributes.same.sense` (29 facts: turkey, chicken, salmon the animal; kiwi, orange,
  apricot the food; lilac, rose, violet, mushroom the plant; bell, cone the thing). Judged by the worker from the
  values (a turkey of 9 kg that runs 40 km/h is the bird; a kiwi of 7 cm the fruit); in every case it is the sense of
  the drawing of the name. Table `sources/hand-core/senses.tsv` (read by `tools/lib/senses.mjs`); `tools/joins.mjs`
  files the fact under that sense instead of `unsure` under all of them.

## When and by whom

- `k.attributes.usage` (identity `name`, Round 10 item 6): for 338 things of `k.scenes`, model-labelled, only where
  plain: `season` (100), `time` of day (177), `weather` (58), with the values of `k.scenes.scene` (a sled fits a
  winter scene with snow; an owl a night scene); `age` (107: baby, child, adult, old, the ages of `k.people.kind`);
  `used_by` (69 facts; a `k.people.role`, only where the role does not already list the thing in its `uses`).
  Written by `tools/usage-core.mjs` from `sources/hand-core/usage.tsv`; audit `usage-audit.tsv`.
- Q37: `sense` as for the numbers, on 5 facts (fox the animal, hay and wheat the food, toast the thing, star the body
  in space: at night).
- The family `scene thing` (the 2,218 things of `k.scenes`) has its row in ASPECTS.md: temporal 257, social 256.

## Typical ranges and typicality (blocks 15:15 item 3, 14:15 item 2, 17:05)

- `k.attributes.range` (identity `type`, `key`, `kind`): the typical range of a number for things of a kind, so that
  "a number far from the usual" can be computed (k.interest `range_distance`: the distance to the nearer end in
  widths of the range). 56 ranges over 34 kinds: **41 from the catalog** (`basis=catalog`: the interquartile range
  and median of at least 8 numbers; the quartile rule of k.interest's test): the size of 13 kinds of common things,
  the weight of 11 and the speed of 2 (k.attributes.number by k.attributes.thing kind), each also for mammals and
  birds (k.animals class); the length
  of body parts, and in k.geo the population of countries and cities, the elevation of mountains, the length of
  rivers, the area of lakes, seas and deserts, and the area of countries (ODbL numbers, shard `ranges-odbl`); **7
  sourced** (`basis=source`, `cite`): the height of men, women and persons (NCD Risk Factor Collaboration 2016,
  eLife 5:e13410: the lowest and highest national means), of babies and children (WHO Child Growth Standards 2006;
  de Onis et al. 2007); **4 own choice** (`basis=own`): a house 5-12 m (from CTBUH's 3.1 m residential storey), a
  skyscraper 150-350 m (CTBUH's classes), the seats of a table 4-10 (`w.seating.table`), the quantity of a list item
  1-6 (`w.list.item`). The unit is the unit k.units.measure gives for the type and key.
- **4 from Wikidata** (CC0, shard `ranges-wikidata`, `basis=source`, source `wikidata-typical 2026-11-14`, fetched
  through PSys2's machine, blocks 17:55 and 19:35): the mass (1,405 taxa) and length (288) of species, the height of
  buildings (1,435), the area of countries (210); one value per item (the median of its statements), units converted.
  They stand beside the catalog's ranges of the same kind (the basis is part of the identity): a catalog range is the
  usual of everyday things (model-labelled numbers; only 11 mammals have one, so the mammal range, 2-3 m, says the
  catalog's mammals are large ones), a Wikidata range the usual of the items that carry the number (most described
  species are small: median 24 g, under the catalog's lower quartile of animals; buildings with a height: 13.5-45 m,
  median 22 m). The rest of the fetched files is not used, with the reasons, in `sources/wikidata-typical/SOURCE.md`;
  no per-item value was imported (5 of the 314 animals of k.animals have a Wikidata mass in the sample).
- `k.attributes.typical` (identity `of`, `attribute`): how usual it is that a thing of a kind has, and a request names,
  a value for an attribute: `weight` in (0, 1], own choice. 143 facts over 35 kinds and types: 18 kinds of common
  things (drawings: a house is a building, size 1, parts 0.8, material 0.5; a person: age 0.8, colour 0.7, size 0.6),
  every kind of the world schemas (`w.seating.guest`: seat 1, name 0.9, party 0.4; `w.map.place`: highlight 1, colour
  0.8; `w.list.item`: text 1, done 0.8, quantity 0.7; the parts of a face through `w.face.part`), and the places of
  the map (`k.geo.country`: capital 1, population 0.9, continent 0.8). Kinds without typicality: creature, machine,
  jewellery, weapon, tableware, part, material, natural thing (few things, few attributes).
- Written by `tools/ranges-core.mjs` (shards `ranges`, `ranges-odbl`, `ranges-wikidata`, `typical`); checked by
  `tests/k.attributes.ranges.test.mjs` (the shards are the tool's output; every range names numbers that exist with
  their unit, a known kind, low < median < high; the elephant is far above the animals' range and a dog inside;
  every typicality names a kind and attribute or a type and its key; coverage of the kinds std.sample hits).

## One name, one thing

- `k.attributes.same` (identity `name`, `sense`): for the 3,330 names that facts of more than one world carry,
  the sense of the name and the types (`in`) whose facts with that name are about that one thing. `only=true`
  (3,262 names): one sense in the whole catalog, so a host may treat the thing as one thing in every view (duck:
  `k.objects.object`, `k.animals.animal`, `k.scenes.thing`, `k.attributes.drawn`). `only=false` (68 names, each
  sense its own fact): orange is a food, a colour and a river; the drawing `crane` is the machine and the
  drawing `kiwi` the fruit, while `k.animals` has the birds; turkey is an animal, a food and a country.
  `by_word` lists the types keyed by the word whatever it means (`k.words.sense`, `k.parts`).
- Computed by `tools/joins.mjs` by rule (the sense of a type; for a drawing, the group its source filed it in);
  shards `same-<letter>`; all clashes in [JOINS.md](../JOINS.md). Rerun with `npm run build` when a world changes.

## Shards

`attributes` (67 wires), `aspects` (153 wires), `things-living` (animals and creatures), `things-nature` (plants, land, sky, materials,
places, food and drink), `things-big` (vehicles, machines, buildings), `things-worn` (clothing, jewellery),
`things-objects` (the rest). Written by `tools/attributes-core.mjs` from `sources/hand-core/attributes.tsv`.
`drawn-hand`, `drawn-mulberry`, `drawn-noto`, `drawn-openmoji` (one per source of drawings, with the licence of
that source), written by `tools/drawn-colour.mjs`. `numbers-a-f`, `numbers-g-o`, `numbers-p-z` (the numbers of
common things), written by `tools/numbers-core.mjs`. `usage` (when and by whom), written by `tools/usage-core.mjs`.
`ranges`, `ranges-odbl` (ODbL-1.0), `ranges-wikidata` and `typical`, written by `tools/ranges-core.mjs`.

## Audit

A seeded sample of 90 rows of the table was read again: 87 right, 0 wrong, 3 unclear (corrected: the material of
a temple, the kind of a roller skate, one empty row). Record: `sources/hand-core/attributes-audit.tsv`.

## What it does not know

- The usual colour of most things: 256 things here and the 154 foods of `k.food`, not the 2,000 asked for. Colours
  that depend on the country or the make were left out (a postbox, a taxi, a barn).
- The drawn colour is only as good as the colour names: a pale cream or a skin tone has no basic colour of its own
  and is given as yellow or brown (the shade says cream, apricot, blanched almond); an outline is not counted.
- Legs, food and habitat of animals are only in `k.animals` (156 animals); nothing was taken from WordNet glosses
  or ConceptNet (ConceptNet is not reachable from the build network).
